Teacher notes, source and playback help

Source: “QUESTION TAGS in MOVIES”, English Thru Clips (9 December 2023). The extracts stream from the original YouTube upload; no footage has been copied or re-uploaded. Film identities are not supplied where they have not been verified. Captions and cue points were prepared from the source’s automatic transcript: preview the extracts before class and use Replay if a cue feels too tight.

The source repeats many examples. This player selects one occurrence of each. The first watch is a listening task; the teaching pause comes afterwards, so students recall and explain the tag rather than predict unseen dialogue. Embedded or original captions may already display the answer.

Standard pattern: positive statement + negative tag; negative statement + positive tag. These selected extracts mainly model positive statements. With “I am”, use “aren’t I?”; with “Let’s”, use “shall we?”. A rising tag often checks information; a falling tag often seeks agreement. Listen to the actual voice rather than assuming a meaning from punctuation alone.

YouTube may show adverts, require consent or restrict embedding. If playback is blocked, use the timestamped YouTube link and run the teaching cards manually. No invideo credits, purchases, accounts or student data are needed. Timing excludes ads and buffering. The classroom host controls pace; this is not an exam.
